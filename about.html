About me

I am an undergraduate student in Electronic Science and Technology at Zhejiang University, affiliated with the Chu Kochen Honors College and the Shannon Excellence Program. I am also part of the Advanced Class of Engineering Education (ACEE).

My research interests lie at the intersection of embodied AI, vision-language-action models, autonomous driving, robotics, and multimodal learning.

Research interests

My current research focuses on the robustness and generalization of end-to-end autonomous driving systems under visual distribution shifts. In particular, I study how appearance changes affect trajectory generation and decision making in VLA-based planners, and how physically grounded evidence can be used to recover reliable planning behavior.

My recent work investigates an appearance robustness recovery framework that decomposes planning degradation into trajectory support failure and decision corruption, and addresses them through Support Recovery and Certified Decision Repair.

More broadly, I am interested in building embodied agents that can ground multimodal perception in physical constraints, reason over possible actions, and generalize reliably beyond their training distributions. My long-term research interests include VLA/VLN systems, world models, robot learning, physical reasoning, and robust decision making.

Background

I have received the National Scholarship and a National First Prize in the Intel Cup Embedded System Design Contest. I am currently exploring graduate research opportunities in robotics, embodied intelligence, and multimodal AI.
